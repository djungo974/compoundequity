# Legal review checklist — for a U.S. securities attorney

All legal pages on the site (`terms.html`, `privacy.html`, `disclaimer.html`, `disclosure.html`, `trading-policy.html`) are drafts written without legal review. The site is not claimed to be compliant. Points to review:

## Regulatory status
1. **Publisher's exclusion (Investment Advisers Act of 1940, §202(a)(11)(D); *Lowe v. SEC*, 1985).** Does the model qualify: impersonal content, the same for all subscribers, published on a regular schedule, general and regular circulation, not timed to specific market activity? Pay particular attention to:
   - **Real-time alerts** (Pro, score-5 signals, sent outside the regular schedule). Could they be seen as "timed" communications that weaken the bona fide publication argument?
   - The **signals dashboard**: is an interactive tool treated differently from a newsletter?
   - The **1–5 importance score**: is it only an editorial ranking of news, or could it be read as a rating or recommendation of securities?
2. **State investment adviser laws**: does any state apply a narrower publisher exemption?
3. **Non-U.S. subscribers**: the target audience is English-speaking, so readers may be in the UK, EU, Canada or Australia. Do their "investment advice" or financial promotion rules apply to a U.S. publisher (for example, the UK financial promotion regime)?
4. **Founder's residence**: the founder is a tax resident of Mauritius and runs a U.S. LLC. Any Mauritian (FSC) licensing questions for publishing investment research from Mauritius?

## Conflicts of interest
5. Is the disclosure format adequate: holdings on a page plus a block in every issue, with funds identified by full name and ISIN?
6. The author holds sector **ETFs**, and those ETFs hold the companies discussed. Is disclosing the ETF positions enough, or should the indirect exposure be quantified?
7. **Personal trading policy**: review the 48-hour blackout and the proposed exception for pre-scheduled investment plans (section 3). With a daily publication, a literal reading would block nearly all trading in the author's funds.
8. Scalping and front-running risk (SEC antifraud rules, Section 10(b) / Rule 10b-5, Section 206 if the exclusion didn't apply): is the policy sufficient protection?

## Commercial terms
9. Auto-renewal disclosure requirements (California and other states' automatic renewal laws, FTC "click-to-cancel" rule status).
10. Refund policy (not decided yet).
11. Founding-member "price locked for life" wording: any consumer-protection issue?
12. Governing law, venue, arbitration or class-action waiver, and consumer carve-outs for non-U.S. subscribers.

## Privacy
13. GDPR/UK GDPR applicability (EU/UK subscribers), cross-border transfer mechanisms, and CCPA/CPRA thresholds.

## Brand and entity
14. LLC name vs. trade name (DBA) filing, and the wording "[BRAND] is a trade name of [LLC]" on the site, in Stripe and in emails.
15. Trademark clearance for the final brand name (classes 36 and 41). "Compound Equity" is not usable: "Compound Equity Group" is an FCA-authorised UK firm.
